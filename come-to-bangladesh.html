Why Bangladesh

A country you feel before you understand it

Rivers, rickshaws, festivals and some of the warmest hospitality in the world.

আতিথেয়তা

Hospitality

Guests are treated like family. Expect to be invited for tea, for dinner, and to more weddings than you can attend.

খাবার

Food

Biryani in Old Dhaka, fresh hilsa fish, street-side fuchka and endless cups of milk tea. You will not go hungry.

প্রকৃতি

Nature

The Sundarbans mangrove forest, the long beach at Cox's Bazar, and the green tea gardens of Sylhet.

উৎসব

Festivals

Celebrate Pohela Boishakh (Bengali New Year), Eid and many local festivals with music, colour and street food.

    Where you'll stay

    Your home in Bangladesh

    Volunteers usually share a furnished apartment or stay with a host family close to their project. You'll live with other international volunteers or local members, so you're never far from help or company.

    • Shared bedroom with a bed, fan and storage
    • Shared kitchen and bathroom
    • Wi-Fi or mobile data support
    • Close to public transport and local shops

    Exact arrangements depend on the project and are listed on each project's aiesec.org page.

    Good to know

    Life in Bangladesh, the practical bits

    Money

    The currency is the Bangladeshi Taka (BDT). Cards work in cities, but carry cash for markets, rickshaws and street food.

    Weather

    Hot and humid most of the year. Monsoon runs roughly June to October; November to February is mild and dry.

    Getting around

    Rickshaws, CNG auto-rickshaws, ride-hailing apps and the Dhaka Metro Rail. Your buddy will show you how.

    Phone & internet

    Get a local SIM on arrival with your passport. Mobile data is affordable and widely available.

    Language

    Bangla is the national language. English is common at universities and workplaces, and a few Bangla words go a long way.

    Culture & dress

    Bangladesh is a Muslim-majority country. Modest clothing that covers shoulders and knees is appreciated.
